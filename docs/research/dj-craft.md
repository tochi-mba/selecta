# How great DJs make decisions and shape a set (notes, November 2026)

Tags are [solid], [practitioner] and [anecdotal]. Vendor pages count as anecdotal. Both measured mix studies are dance-music heavy, so nothing measured covers pop, rap, Latin or Asian pop. I could not retrieve usable text from RA's Art of DJing, RBMA lectures or How to DJ Right. No files were written, and I used well over the 15-call guideline because early results were mostly vendor content.

## 1. Variables and what matters most

### Takeaway
Fit for the moment comes first. Timbre and tempo continuity show up in real mixes, and key is a situational tool whose value grows with overlap length.

### Cited Findings
- **Essential Mix order (114 sets):** real order had smaller timbre jumps than a shuffle. Median was 1.02 against 1.13, with random EDM at 1.39 and albums at 0.80. Mean loudness change was 0.76 dB against 3.18 for random EDM. Key showed no significant effect. Caveat: 2-minute excerpts, some falling within one track. [solid] [Kell & Tzanetakis 2013](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2013/paper/000210.pdf)
- **Stretch and transposition (1,557 mixes, 20,765 transitions, mostly house and trance):** 86.1% of tracks were stretched under 5%, 94.5% under 10%, 98.6% under 20%. Only 2.5% were transposed, and 94.3% of those by one semitone. [solid] [Kim et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.10267)
- **Consonance over key:** trained listeners preferred alignments chosen by measured consonance over a key-based commercial tool's picks. [solid, abstract only] [Gebhardt et al.](https://repositorio.inesctec.pt/handle/123456789/3896)
- **Stretch discomfort:** excess stretching causes discomfort, and so do half/double errors that put strong beats on weak ones. I could not get the thresholds. [solid, abstract only] [Ishizaki et al. 2009](https://zenodo.org/records/1418231/files/IshizakiHT09.pdf)
- **Right record first:** Tony Corless says the next record "has to be the right one first and foremost". Key mainly buys longer blends. He cites about 60% key-software accuracy in DJTT tests (2010, dated). Ean Golden keeps key while the floor works and drops it when it doesn't. [practitioner] [DJTT](https://djtechtools.com/2010/03/23/to-key-or-not-to-key/)
- **Vendor's own limits:** Mixed In Key says key matters most for vocals, pads and long blends. Cuts and drum-only moves can ignore it. [anecdotal, vendor] [MIK](https://mixedinkey.com/workflows/how-to-mix-in-key/)
- **Selection over technique:** selection beats technical mixing, and a well-timed cut can reset a room if rare. [practitioner] [Santos](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/2016/09/6-top-mistakes-beginner-djs-make/)
- **Production as navigation:** BPM matching is not the goal for DJ88, who uses production feel to move between genres. [practitioner] [Mixcloud](https://www.mixcloud.com/blog/2025/11/14/how-to-master-open-format-dj-sets-with-dj88)
- **Classics:** crowds care less about new releases than DJs do, so keep classics in rotation. [practitioner] [Hricik](https://djtechtools.com/2018/03/25/5-mistakes-new-club-djs-make-and-how-to-avoid-them/)

### Inferences
- My ranking is moment fit, then timbre, tempo feasibility, vocal clearance, then key. No source ranks all five.
- The key evidence is contested. Kell's null may reflect estimator noise, and low transposition may only mean DJs pick compatible tracks.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative weighting of era or familiarity.

## 2. Phrasing

### Takeaway
Entries land on structural boundaries and overlaps snap to phrase multiples. Evidence is good for dance music, loose for pop and hip-hop, and thin elsewhere.

### Cited Findings
- **Measured phrase locking:** transition lengths peak every 32 beats. Cue-outs peak at 32-beat offsets from annotated boundaries, and cue-ins skew to zero offset. [solid] [Kim](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.10267)
- **Cue agreement between DJs on the same track:** 23.6% identical, 40.4% within 1 bar, 73.6% within 8, 86.2% within 16. [solid] [Kim](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.10267)
- **Golden on boundaries (all [practitioner], [DJTT](https://djtechtools.com/2009/01/26/phrasing-the-perfect-mix/)):**
  - Start the incoming track at the outgoing track's phrase start. Boundaries are audible as a new instrument, crash or fill.
  - Pop and hip-hop have 16-count intros and 32- or 64-count choruses. "Counts" probably means beats, but he doesn't define it.
  - Leave from the chorus so the next verse starts as it ends.
  - Don't cut before the payoff, but intro-outro-only mixing drains energy.
- **General pattern:** 16-bar sections, 4–8 bar builds, 8–16 bar breakdowns, with hip-hop phrases shorter than house. [anecdotal, vendor] [dj.studio](https://dj.studio/blog/phrasing-dj-mixing)
- **Hip-hop specifics:**
  - 8-bar sections are common. [anecdotal] [DJTT forum](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/tips-for-mixing-top-40/49480)
  - Double hooks by jumping back 16 bars, and loop outros into the next first verse. [practitioner] [Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/how-to-mix-hip-hop/)
- **No-intro songs:** loop 4–16 beats of drums, snap at a major phrase change, do a beatless melodic blend, or FX out. [practitioner] [Russel](https://djtechtools.com/2017/09/11/mixing-songs-dont-dj-intro-4-common-techniques/)
- **Latin:**
  - Downbeats and bar counts are harder to find in salsa, merengue and bachata. The editor admits he doesn't mix Latin. [anecdotal] [DDJT](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/over-to-you-struggling-to-mix-latin-music)
  - For social dancing: no mixing, no cutting, keep BPM, 3–4 seconds of silence. Beatmatching suits reggaeton and Latin house. [anecdotal] [Philipp](https://socialdancecommunity.com/latin-music-dj-your-guide-to-becoming-a-world-class-salsa-and-bachata-mixer/)
- **Afrobeats and amapiano (unattributed, partly auto-generated):**
  - Afrobeats: median 110 BPM, 8/16-bar blocks, blends of at least 16 bars. The same page also gives 4–8 bars. [anecdotal] [Mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/bpm-for/afrobeats)
  - Amapiano: median 113 BPM, 16–32 bar blends. [anecdotal] [Mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/bpm-for/amapiano)

### Inferences
- Cue agreement implies several valid cues per track, so software should generate and score candidates rather than hunt for one correct point.

### Gaps
- I found no practitioner source on reggaeton, dembow, Afrobeats, or K-pop, C-pop or J-pop phrasing.
- Irregular 6- and 10-bar phrases appeared only in an untraced search snippet. Detect phrases from audio.

## 3. Genre and big BPM jumps

### Takeaway
DJs change genre with a musical link plus a clean break, and they step tempo rather than stretch it far.

### Cited Findings
- **Three core moves:** exit in a breakdown; echo out at a phrase end (1-beat echo, usually at 16 bars); or ramp a vocal-free 4-beat loop to the new tempo. [practitioner] [Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/3-easy-transitions-to-mix-any-genre/)
- **Bridges:** bridge via vibe, production or wordplay. If a mix fails, regroup instead of panic-grabbing a similar BPM. [practitioner] [DJ88](https://www.mixcloud.com/blog/2025/11/14/how-to-master-open-format-dj-sets-with-dj88)
- **Coherence:** eclectic sets need links (vocals, instrumentation, energy, tempo, key, groove), mood blocks and an overriding theme. [practitioner] [Morse](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/?p=26394)
- **Wide gaps:** a beatless melodic overlap in the same key handled 99 against 120 BPM. [practitioner] [Russel](https://djtechtools.com/2017/09/11/mixing-songs-dont-dj-intro-4-common-techniques/)
- **Forum methods:** [anecdotal] [DJTT forum](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/tips-for-mixing-top-40/49480)
  - Close a 10–15 BPM gap across one or two songs.
  - Use a tempo ladder rather than bouncing back and forth.
  - Treat 65–70 BPM hip-hop as halftime against 128–130 house.
  - Slam, brake or echo-freeze into known tracks.
  - Practitioners split on transition tracks: one calls them a crutch, another finds them useful.
- **Tempo progression:** hip-hop to pop to house to electro to dubstep to drum and bass over about two hours, then repeat. A 170-to-110 reset is simply dropped unmixed, and hip-hop works as a rest. [anecdotal] [DJTT forum](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/designing-sets-that-include-alot-of-genres/39545)
- **Bridge tracks:** a low-BPM high-energy or high-BPM chilled track disguises tempo moves. Pitch-locked synced acapellas can bridge too. [practitioner] [Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/how-to-mix-hip-hop/)
- **Step sizes:** 124 to 128 reads as a lift, while 124 to 140 reads as a genre change. [anecdotal, vendor] [Mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/energy-flow-guide)

### Inferences
- "BPM isn't the goal" and the small measured stretches fit together if big jumps are broken rather than blended.

### Gaps
- I found no measured data on how often genre jumps occur or how they land.

## 4. How long each track plays

### Takeaway
There is no standard, and reports run from under a minute to the full song. The best open-format anchor is two verses and a chorus, with verse-chorus-out reserved for high-energy bursts.

### Cited Findings
- **Dancefloor default:** about two verses and a chorus unless a sing-along moment is coming; in a bar or lounge, most of the song; never leave mid-line. [practitioner] [DJ88](https://www.mixcloud.com/blog/2025/11/14/how-to-master-open-format-dj-sets-with-dj88)
- **Quick mixing:** suits high-energy moments, not warm-ups or a whole set, which becomes tiring, and YouTube quick-mix videos are built for views. It also suits spent tracks. A commenter plays one verse and chorus, adding a second of each for new or huge hits. [practitioner] [Morse](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/should-djs-play-the-whole-track/)
- **Reported per-song times:** [anecdotal] [DJTT forum](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/rapid-fire-song-mixing-vs-start-to-finish-mixing/69958), [DJTT forum](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/at-a-club-how-much-of-a-song-do-you-typically-play/21548)
  - One minute or less for commercial DJs in one town.
  - 60–90 seconds, which the poster later said was mainly to show off.
  - 2–2¼ minutes in clubs, down from three.
  - 2:30–3:30 for mobile.
  - 4–5 minutes when letting tracks run.
- **Planning figures:** 18–22 tracks per hour, 2.5–3.5 minutes each, 30–60 second overlaps. No evidence offered. [anecdotal, vendor] [Mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/dj-set-planning-guide)
- **Dissent:**
  - Ben UFO lets tracks play out and rarely edits, and a peer says he favours tracks that can run six or seven minutes. [practitioner] [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/feature/pure-and-simple-ben-ufo-is-one-of-the-most-beloved-djs-of-our-time)
  - Party DJ Stu Chisholm plays songs whole, cutting only failures. [practitioner] [Pro Mobile](https://promobiledj.com/2013/02/mixing-how-much-is-too-much)
- **Live disagreement on familiarity:** one DJ gives unknown tracks longer and big tunes only intro-verse-chorus, while another lets hot songs ride. Lukewarm tracks leave after the first verse. Radio and podcast mixes run fuller songs. [anecdotal] [DJTT forum](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/rapid-fire-song-mixing-vs-start-to-finish-mixing/69958), [DJTT forum](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/at-a-club-how-much-of-a-song-do-you-typically-play/21548)

### Inferences
- Dance mixes: 60.5 minutes over 15.5 identified tracks gives at most 3.9 minutes per slot, near the 4.0-minute average track in [Kim](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.10267). Unidentified tracks inflate this.
- Open format on 3-minute songs probably plays 60–85%. That is my estimate, not a measurement.

### Gaps
- I found no measured play time for open format or hip-hop, and no source for a radio mix-show norm such as 45 seconds per song.

## 5. Shaping a set

### Takeaway
Sets move in blocks and waves, with small even steps punctuated by deliberate contrast. Nearly all the numbers are unsupported heuristics.

### Cited Findings
- **Equal steps:** median-distance and equal-step orderings matched real order better than ranking by similarity to the first track, which supports transitions of roughly equal size. [solid, EDM only] [Kell](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2013/paper/000210.pdf)
- **Give and take:** Jazzy Jeff describes a journey with give and take, keeps 50–60 themed set folders, and values fresh surprises. [practitioner] [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/2013/04/interview-dj-jazzy-jeff-on-preparing-for-a-party-and-the-state-of-rb)
- **Pacing the room:** plan in blocks, build peaks and troughs, and hold the biggest tunes out of the first hour of a long set. The same thing for half an hour risks losing the room. Use a throwback as a wild card, not a genre opposite. [practitioner] [Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/how-to-read-a-crowd-as-a-dj/)
- **Duration of a groove:** hold 30–60 minutes for commercial crowds, or repeat a 1.5–2 hour block as crowds turn over. Staying in one tempo too long dulls the floor. [anecdotal] [DJTT forum](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/tips-for-mixing-top-40/49480)
- **Arc numbers, no evidence offered:** [anecdotal, vendor] [Mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/dj-set-planning-guide)
  - Open at 60–70% of peak.
  - Blocks of 3–4 tracks.
  - Peak of 3–5 tracks about two-thirds through.
  - Dip 10–15% for 2–3 tracks, then a smaller second peak.
  - Steps of about 1 BPM.
  - 2–3 tracks per key.
  - Land over the last 3–4 tracks.
- **Fatigue and resets:** 15 high-energy tracks in a row feels exhausting, and resets should be limited to once per set. [anecdotal, vendor] [Mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/energy-flow-guide)
- **Warm-ups:** three 30-minute phases spanning 4–8 BPM, attributed to unnamed pros. [anecdotal] [Dubspot](https://blog.dubspot.com/tips-for-opening-djs-josh-wink-hector-romero-mike-huckaby-dj-dan-shiftee)

### Inferences
- Smoothness and surprise are not opposed. Ben UFO likes abrupt contrasts but mixes them with the groove ([Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/feature/pure-and-simple-ben-ufo-is-one-of-the-most-beloved-djs-of-our-time)).

### Gaps
- I found no source at all on callbacks.

## 6. Great versus average, and common mistakes

### Takeaway
Practitioners credit selection, listening and restraint. Amateur mixes are usually marked by clashes and by cutting at the wrong moments.

### Cited Findings
- **Selection:** Z-Trip stresses selecting and reading a crowd (search snippet only, page text unavailable). [practitioner] [Tranzfusion](https://www.tranzfusion.net/music/features/2006/ZTrip_interview_Crowd_Rocker)
- **Listening:** Ben UFO treats beatmatching as a base that frees attention for flow and why grooves complement each other. [practitioner] [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/feature/pure-and-simple-ben-ufo-is-one-of-the-most-beloved-djs-of-our-time)
- **Named mistakes:** a rigid set, opening with bangers, technique over selection, ignoring the crowd. [practitioner] [Santos](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/2016/09/6-top-mistakes-beginner-djs-make/)
- **Vocal overlap:** 4–8 bars of both vocals is manageable, 32 is a mess, and 16 bars of two rap tracks almost always fails. Rap almost never blends. Let vocals take turns, and let the outgoing vocal finish its phrase. Mixgraph flags these as rules of thumb. [anecdotal, vendor] [Mixgraph](https://www.mixgraph.io/learn/vocal-mixing-guide)
- **Overreacting:** reacting to every bit of crowd movement disrupts a floor that was fine. [practitioner] [Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/how-to-read-a-crowd-as-a-dj/)
- **Rigid formats:** a known set folder or fixed sequence fails once the room responds differently; Corless predicts a pre-written 20-track set won't survive past track five. [practitioner] [DJTT](https://djtechtools.com/2010/03/23/to-key-or-not-to-key/)

### Gaps
- All of this is opinion. I found no blind test of mix quality by DJ experience.

## 7. Stand-ins for reading a crowd

### Takeaway
DJs read trends across several tracks, not single reactions. A single listener's skips, steering and history can stand in, but imperfectly.

### Cited Findings
- **Skip data:** Spotify plays show 24.14% skipped within 5 seconds and 35.05% within 30, with 48.6% abandoned before the end. Engaged listeners skip more, so a skip isn't pure dislike (2014 aggregate). [solid] [Lamere](https://musicmachinery.com/2014/05/02/the-skip/)
- **DJ signals:** dancing, nodding, movement toward the floor, repeated artist requests, and testing styles early in a set. [practitioner] [DDJT](https://digitaldjtips.com/?p=14683)
- **Timescale:** judge over a handful of tracks and treat each track as an experiment. [practitioner] [Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/how-to-read-a-crowd-as-a-dj/)

### Gaps
- I found no study validating any proxy for solo listening.

## Design implications for Selecta

These are my proposals. "Evidence" marks numbers tied to a study above, and the rest are starting guesses to tune by ear.

**Next-track score (guessed weights)**

| Term | Weight |
|---|---|
| Arc and energy fit | 0.25 |
| Timbre step | 0.20 |
| Transition feasibility | 0.20 |
| Tempo | 0.15 |
| Affinity and steering | 0.10 |
| Freshness | 0.10 |

**Key**
- Key is not a top-level term. It is a penalty inside feasibility: zero for overlaps of 2 bars or less or drums-only, partial at 4–8 bars, full at 16+ with melodic or vocal content.
- Since Selecta renders offline, measure roughness on the actual overlap (Gebhardt). Reject a blend on roughness, never a track on Camelot alone.

**Timbre**
- Aim for a neighbour distance of 0.6–0.85 × the library's median random-pair distance. Kell's ratios give about 0.73–0.81 for DJ sets and about 0.6 for albums (evidence, EDM only).
- Penalise too-close pairs and any variance in step size. Allow larger steps only at block changes.

**Tempo**
- Compare across ×0.5, ×1 and ×2.
- Stretch is free up to 5%, penalised from 5–10%, and no beatmatched blend above 10% (evidence, Kim).
- 2:1 mixes require bar-one alignment (Ishizaki).
- Within a block, drift 1–2 BPM per transition (vendor).

**Transition choice**

| Condition | Move | Overlap |
|---|---|---|
| Within 5%, house/dance, instrumental windows | Long blend | 16–32 bars |
| Within 5%, pop/R&B | Chorus-out into intro or verse, vocals taking turns | 4–8 bars |
| Rap into rap | Cut on bar one, or intro under outro | At most 2–4 bars of both vocals |
| 5–10% | Short blend or ramp over 1–2 tracks | 4–8 bars |
| Exact 2:1 | Halftime blend, downbeats aligned | 8 bars |
| Over 10%, no 2:1 | Echo-out at phrase end or breakdown exit, next track on bar one | 1–2 bar tail |
| Low downbeat confidence (salsa, merengue, live drums) | FX-out or clean cut | At most 2 bars |
| Salsa/bachata block (optional) | Full play, no stretch, 3–4 s gap | None |

**Phrasing**
- Detect phrases per track from audio, and never assume a grid.
- Store 3–5 entry and 3–5 exit cues per track.
- Lock entries within 1 bar of a boundary.
- For dance music, snap overlaps to 8/16/32 bars (evidence, Kim).

**Hard gates**
- Both lead vocals audible for at most 8 bars when sung and 2–4 when rap.
- No exit mid-word or mid-line.
- No exit before one full chorus has played.

**Length modes**

| Mode | Structure | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Club (default) | Exit after second chorus | 1:45–3:00 |
| Burst | Verse and chorus, only at energy 0.8 or above, at most 3–4 in a row | 1:00–1:45 |
| Radio/listening | Exit at outro or final chorus | 3:00 to full |
| House/dance | Most of the track | 3:30–5:00 |

Add a section for unknown tracks or top-affinity tracks, and trim tracks you've recently overplayed. This takes a side in the forum disagreement. Avoid the same mode more than four times running, since contrast is the point.

**Arc (60-minute wave)**
- Open at 0.6–0.7 of peak, in plateaus of 3–4 tracks.
- Peak for 3–5 tracks at about 60–70% through.
- Dip 10–15% for 2–3 tracks, reach a smaller second peak, and land over 3–4 tracks.
- Longer sessions repeat the wave with a rest block, such as hip-hop or R&B.

**Blocks and fatigue**
- 4–8 tracks per genre family, with genre switches only at block ends.
- Require a shared link at a switch: timbre, vocal type, era or groove.
- Force some change by 30 minutes (Hartley).
- At most 6–8 tracks at energy 0.8 or above, and one hard reset per wave.
- Keep loudness change near or under 1 dB (evidence, Kell).

**Familiarity**
- 50–70% of tracks familiar by play count.
- One throwback wild card per 8–12 tracks.
- Artist separation of 10 tracks or more.
- Callbacks have no evidence, so cap them at one hook-double per hour, at a peak, for top-affinity tracks only.

**Crowd proxies**
- A skip within 30 seconds is strongly negative, so cool that track.
- A skip after 60% heard is only a mild signal.
- One skip never moves the arc. Two skips in five tracks sharing a genre, era or energy pivots within 1–2 tracks through a bridge.
- Treat steering as a request, and honour it within 1–2 tracks via a bridge.
- Use the first 3–5 tracks as gentle probes.
- Log completions, replays, queue adds, volume changes and clock hour.

**Fallback**
- If nothing clears the score threshold, echo out deliberately into a high-affinity track rather than taking the nearest BPM (DJ88).

**Audit each rendered set**
- At least 85% of transitions stretched 5% or less.
- All cues on detected boundaries.
- No mid-word exits.
- Vocal overlap within the limits above.
