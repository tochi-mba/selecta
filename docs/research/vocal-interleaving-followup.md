# Vocal interleaving follow-up - 6 November 2026

## Sources and their limits

[Native Instruments' phrase-mixing guide](https://www.native-instruments.com/blogs/blog/phrase-mixing)
describes using arrangement and song structure to choose transitions, including layering vocals,
instruments and drums. Phrase alignment is therefore part of choosing the material, not merely
aligning beats after a move has been selected. This is instructional guidance, not an evaluation of
Selecta's detector or scoring weights.

[Algoriddim's Neural Mix crossfader manual](https://help.algoriddim.com/user-manual/djay-pro-windows/neural-mix/crossfaders)
documents independent stem swaps and cuts. It supports offering separate stem decisions, but does
not establish which automatic arrangement will sound best.

[This Beatmatch discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Beatmatch/comments/1129ufl/transition_overlapping_vocals/)
contains competing practitioner advice: avoid vocal collisions; choose later cues; use loops into
breaks; or deliberately interleave complementary phrases. One participant distinguishes abstract
singing from competing lyrics and recommends trying exceptional pairings in advance. These are
anecdotes, not calibrated thresholds or proof that simultaneous vocals usually work.

## Consequences for Selecta - hypotheses to test

- Treat competing intelligible phrases differently from a sustained background vocal. Current
  stem-energy measurements cannot reliably make that distinction; do not weaken collision checks
  solely because a forum describes an exception.
- Search alternative entry and exit phrases before changing effects. A good stem shape on one cue
  pair is not evidence that the same shape suits another section of the same songs.
- Keep user-approved exceptions scoped to their pair, placement and shape. Broader preference
  learning should propose candidates for audition rather than silently turning an exception into
  a general permission to overlap vocals.
- Evaluate interleaving against a conventional transition with the same song context. Include
  lyric intelligibility, cut-off phrases, energy continuity and stem artifacts in listener notes.
  An energy or pitch score alone cannot prove these outcomes.

The current eight-cell arranger measures levels, pitch-class profiles and vocal boundary energy.
These sources support investigating finer vocal phrase structure; they do not validate its fixed
weights, the learned energy arc, or the general quality of its output. Human evaluation remains open.
